# Questionnaire (template)

**What the CP4 instructions expect this to hold:** a usability score, for which the instructions suggest SUS or UMUX-Lite, plus satisfaction and what people say about usefulness, trust, and frustrations.

Used only if we pick Track A (decision 1 in [DECISIONS.md](../cp4/DECISIONS.md)). Copy it to `questionnaire.md` first. In the copy, keep one of the two scales below and delete the other.

Keep a scale's statements word for word and in order, since its scoring depends on both. "The system" means the tool the participant just used.

## Scale A: SUS, the System Usability Scale

Ten statements. The participant rates each one from 1 (strongly disagree) to 5 (strongly agree).

1. I think that I would like to use this system frequently.
2. I found the system unnecessarily complex.
3. I thought the system was easy to use.
4. I think that I would need the support of a technical person to be able to use this system.
5. I found the various functions in this system were well integrated.
6. I thought there was too much inconsistency in this system.
7. I would imagine that most people would learn to use this system very quickly.
8. I found the system very cumbersome to use.
9. I felt very confident using the system.
10. I needed to learn a lot of things before I could get going with this system.

Scoring: for statements 1, 3, 5, 7, and 9, take the rating minus 1. For statements 2, 4, 6, 8, and 10, take 5 minus the rating. Add the ten numbers and multiply by 2.5. The score runs from 0 to 100.

Source: Brooke, J. (1996). SUS: A "quick and dirty" usability scale. In P. W. Jordan, B. Thomas, B. A. Weerdmeester, & I. L. McClelland (Eds.), Usability evaluation in industry (pp. 189-194). Taylor & Francis.

## Scale B: UMUX-Lite

Two statements. The participant rates each one from 1 (strongly disagree) to 7 (strongly agree).

1. This system's capabilities meet my requirements.
2. This system is easy to use.

Scoring: add the two ratings, subtract 2, divide by 12, and multiply by 100. The score runs from 0 to 100. The paper also gives an adjusted score that lines up with SUS: 0.65 times that score, plus 22.9. Say in the report which one we used.

Source: Lewis, J. R., Utesch, B. S., & Maher, D. E. (2013). UMUX-LITE: When there's no time for the SUS. Proceedings of the SIGCHI Conference on Human Factors in Computing Systems, 2099-2102.
